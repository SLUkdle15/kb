# Start Investing

Area: [[areas/personal-finance/personal-finance|Personal Finance]]

## Action

Learn enough to make an investing decision on purpose, then make it. Two steps, in order — the second one is worthless without the first, and the first is procrastination without the second.

**1. Learn the fundamentals.** Not stock picking. The parts that actually decide outcomes: time horizon, risk tolerance, diversification, fees, and why low-cost index funds beat active selection for most people. Enough to read a product sheet and know what is being sold.

Reading, in order, queued in [[resources/reading/reading|Reading]]:

- *The Psychology of Money* (Housel) — **start here.** The first failure is behavioral, not analytical, and this is the one that gets finished.
- *The Little Book of Common Sense Investing* (Bogle) — the actual mechanics and the fee argument. US examples, universal conclusion.
- *Millionaire Teacher* (Hallam) — written for people investing from outside the US, which the other two assume away.
- *The Four Pillars of Investing* (Bernstein) — the deep one. Only if the first three land.

No book covers the Vietnamese side, so do not wait for one. Local mechanics — Fmarket and the domestic fund platforms, VN30 ETFs, brokerages like SSI, VPS, and TCBS, capital-gains and dividend tax treatment, and what offshore access actually costs — are research, not reading, and belong in step 2.

**2. Decide and act.** What monthly amount, through which platform, into what. From Vietnam this needs its own answers — VND versus USD exposure, local brokerages and fund platforms, tax treatment, and how it compares to the default habits here of term deposits, gold, and property.

Open questions to settle before any money moves: is there an emergency fund first, and what is this money actually for (a horizon under three years is not an investing question).

## Done When

Parked, not scheduled. Step 2 is multi-step with a real finish line, so promote it to a project when the learning is done and an amount is decided — not before.

Deliberately after [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/score-ielts-7-overall|Score IELTS 7.0 Overall]]. Starting a second learning track during a six-week test sprint would cost the sprint and gain little; the money is not going anywhere.
