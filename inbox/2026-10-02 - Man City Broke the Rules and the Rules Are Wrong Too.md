# Man City Broke the Rules and the Rules Are Wrong Too

From a Mark Goldbridge take on the Man City case. Both halves are needed; either one alone is the weaker argument.

- City broke the rules, and did it to buy power. That was wrong, and the defence that everyone would have done the same does not clear them.
- The rules themselves are not neutral. Spending caps tied to a club's own revenue freeze the existing order: the clubs already earning most are allowed to spend most. A smaller club cannot outspend its way up, because the ceiling is its own turnover.
- So the rules protect incumbents. "Fair play" that locks in who is already big is not fairness, it is a moat with a fairness label on it.
- The league is unfair before anyone cheats. City's breach and the system's unfairness are two separate wrongs, and admitting the first does not settle the second.

Open question: what would a rule look like that stops a club buying a title without also guaranteeing the same six clubs finish on top? Hard cap in absolute money, not revenue-linked? Redistribution of TV money?
