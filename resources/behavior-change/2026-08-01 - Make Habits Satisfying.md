# Make Habits Satisfying

## Core Idea

What is immediately rewarded gets repeated; what is immediately punished gets avoided. Tactics for the 4th law ([[resources/behavior-change/2026-07-29 - Four Laws of Behavior Change|Four Laws of Behavior Change]]) work by adding a little immediate pleasure to habits that pay off long-term, and a little immediate pain to ones that don't.

## Useful Details

- Turn restraint into a reward: whenever you pass on a purchase (skip the morning latte, skip a month of a subscription), move the same amount of money into a savings account.
- Use a habit tracker to measure progress; automate measurement where possible.
- Limit manual tracking to your most important habit(s) — consistently tracking one habit beats sporadically tracking ten.
- Record each measurement immediately after the habit occurs.
- Measurement is only useful when it guides you and adds context — each number is one piece of feedback in the larger system, not something to be consumed by.
- A habit contract adds a social cost to a behavior, making the cost of breaking your promise public and painful — useful for stopping a bad habit.

## Questions This Answers

- Having a reward system: is this defined or spontaneous? — defined: pair the habit with an immediate, tangible payoff (e.g. a savings transfer) rather than waiting for the long-term payoff.
- How to log my performance? and is this act as a craving factor? — use a habit tracker, automate it, track only what matters most, and log right after the habit — the act of logging is itself a small immediate reward.
- How do I remove a bad habit? — partially: add an immediate cost (financial or social, via a habit contract) to make the bad habit immediately unsatisfying.
