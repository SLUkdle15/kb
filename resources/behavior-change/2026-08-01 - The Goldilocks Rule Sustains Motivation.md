# The Goldilocks Rule Sustains Motivation

## Core Idea

Maximum motivation occurs when facing a challenge of just manageable difficulty — not too hard, not too easy. Anyone can work hard while feeling motivated; what separates lasting habits is the ability to keep going when the work isn't exciting.

## Useful Details

- If a habit plateaus, the challenge is likely out of the manageable-difficulty zone — too easy to stay engaging, or too hard to sustain — recalibrate the difficulty rather than relying on motivation alone.
- Reflection and review is a periodic process for staying conscious of your performance over time, distinct from day-to-day habit tracking ([[resources/behavior-change/2026-08-01 - Make Habits Satisfying|Make Habits Satisfying]]).

## Questions This Answers

- When a habit plateaus, what do I do? — recalibrate the challenge back to just-manageable difficulty; don't wait to feel motivated again.
- How do I build a habit that lasts, through bad days and not just good ones? — persistence through unexciting days, plus periodic reflection and review to stay aware of how it's actually going.
