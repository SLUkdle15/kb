# Make Habits Easy

## Core Idea

It is easy to get bogged down trying to find the optimal plan for change — the fastest way to lose weight, the best program to build muscle, the perfect side-hustle idea — and never get around to taking action. Simply putting in your reps is one of the most critical steps to encoding a new habit ([[resources/behavior-change/2026-07-29 - Four Laws of Behavior Change|3rd law]]).

## Useful Details

- Rather than trying to overcome the friction in your life, reduce it — practice environment design.
- Prime the environment for future use.
- Decisive moments: a handful of small choices each day (cook vs. takeout, bike vs. drive, homework vs. game) carry outsized impact — they're forks in the road that steer the rest of the day.
- Two-Minute Rule: a new habit should take less than two minutes to do at the start, staying below the point where it feels like work.
- Automation and commitment devices (strategic one-time decisions, technology) can make good habits inevitable and bad habits impossible, locking in future behavior instead of relying on willpower in the moment.

## Questions This Answers

- Is repetition alone enough to see myself change, or is something else needed? — partially: reps encode the habit where planning alone doesn't; reducing friction is the other lever.
- How to optimize/prime my env? — reduce friction rather than fight it; prime the environment for future use.
- How do I build a habit that lasts, through bad days and not just good ones? — partially: automation and commitment devices carry the habit through days willpower alone wouldn't.
