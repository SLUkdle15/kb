# Self-Kindness in Practice

Source: [[resources/2026-06-09 - Self-Compassion Response to Emotional Triggers]]

## Core Idea

Self-kindness means more than stopping self-judgment — it means actively comforting myself the way I would a dear friend in need, both through physical touch and by reframing the critical inner voice.

## Supportive Touch

Put my hand on my chest, hug myself, or hold my arms gently, speaking in kind, sympathetic tones.

Remind myself:

- "This is really difficult right now. Poor darling."
- "I am here for myself."
- "I do not need to attack myself to be safe."

This works because physical touch activates the body's care-and-soothe system — the same biological response as being comforted by someone who cares about me.

## Reframing Critical Self-Talk

Changing critical self-talk can be practiced as an internal dialogue over several weeks, moving from critic to boundary to compassion. Example:

**Critic:** _"I'm so bad at this. I paid for lessons, why can't I even beat them? What's wrong with me?"_

**Boundary:** _"I know you want me to improve fast, but tearing me down after every loss isn't helping — ease up."_

**Compassion:** _"You've only been playing a few months. Losing to someone with more experience isn't failure, it's just where you are right now. You're getting better each time you play."_
