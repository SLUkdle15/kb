# Common Humanity Loosens Fixed Self-Labels

## Core Idea

Common humanity: the pain I feel in difficult times is the same pain that you feel in difficult times. Applied to a trait I judge myself for, that interconnectedness lets go of the self-definition — the trait stops being "who I am" and becomes something that shows up under certain conditions.

## Interrogating a Trait

- **Frequency** — How often does this trait actually show up? When it's not showing up, are you still you?
- **Conditions** — What situations bring it out, and what situations don't? If it needs specific conditions to appear, is it really "who you are"?
- **Origin** — What caused this trait in the first place (upbringing, pressures, genetics)? If outside forces shaped it, is it really the "true" inner you?
- **Choice** — Did you choose to have this trait? Do you have much control over displaying it? If not, why judge yourself for it?
- **Reframe** — Say it differently: not "I am X," but "Sometimes, in certain circumstances, I do X." Does that create more space, freedom, or peace of mind?

**Reframe:** Instead of _"I'm half-hearted,"_ → _"I stick with things better when I set a clear goal."_

## Questions This Answers

- Why does judging myself for a trait assume the trait is fixed?
- How do I turn "I am X" into something I can live with?
