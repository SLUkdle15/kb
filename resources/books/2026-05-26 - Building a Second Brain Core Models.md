# Building a Second Brain - Core Models

**Source:** *Building a Second Brain* by Tiago Forte

## Summary

A practical method for organizing digital information so you can reduce mental overload, reuse ideas, and produce better creative work.

## Core Philosophy

Your brain is for having ideas, not storing them.

A second brain is an external system for capturing, organizing, and developing knowledge.

The goal is not to save everything. The goal is to save what is useful, surprising, inspiring, or actionable.

Information becomes valuable when it helps you create, decide, solve problems, or express ideas.

## Main Framework: CODE

### Capture

Save ideas, notes, quotes, tasks, and insights that resonate with you.

### Organize

Sort information by actionability, not by topic. Put notes where they will be useful later.

### Distill

Highlight and summarize the most important parts so future you can understand them quickly.

### Express

Turn saved knowledge into output: writing, projects, decisions, presentations, products, or conversations.

## Key Operating Principles

### 12 Favorite Problems

Keep a list of important questions you regularly think about. Capture ideas that help answer them.

### PARA

Organize information into four categories:

- **Projects:** Active work with a clear outcome.
- **Areas:** Long-term responsibilities to maintain.
- **Resources:** Useful topics or references.
- **Archives:** Inactive items kept for future reference.

### Progressive Summarization

Layer meaning over time. First save the note, then bold key points, then highlight the best parts, then summarize the essence.

### Intermediate Packets

Break work into reusable pieces. A note, outline, quote, diagram, checklist, or draft can become building blocks for future projects.

### Expression Has 3 Stages

Start with scattered ideas. Connect them into structure. Then refine them into a finished product.

### Archipelago of Ideas

Build links between ideas before writing. Create islands of thought, then connect them into a clear argument or narrative.

### The Hemingway Bridge

Before stopping work, leave notes for your future self about what to do next. This makes restarting easier.

### Divergence and Convergence

First collect and explore many ideas. Then narrow, select, and shape them into something useful.

## Application

### Externalizing Thoughts

Write ideas down so your mind stays clear and focused.

### Weekly Review

Clean inboxes, update projects, organize notes, and choose priorities.

### Monthly Review

Review goals, areas of responsibility, useful notes, and long-term direction.

## Practical Checklist

- Capture only what feels useful or meaningful.
- Organize notes by where they will be used.
- Keep active projects separate from reference material.
- Review and clean your system regularly.
- Highlight the most important parts of notes.
- Turn notes into small reusable packets.
- Link related ideas together.
- Keep a list of recurring questions.
- Leave clear next steps before stopping work.
- Use saved knowledge to create output.
