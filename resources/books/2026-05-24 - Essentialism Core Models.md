# Essentialism - Core Models

Source: Essentialism by Greg McKeown

Related: [[resources/books/2026-05-26 - Building a Second Brain Core Models|Building a Second Brain Core Models]]

## Summary

Essentialism is the disciplined pursuit of less but better: choosing the few commitments that matter most, accepting trade-offs, eliminating nonessentials, and making execution easier.

## Core Philosophy

The disciplined pursuit of less but better.

This is not just minimalism or doing less. The important part is disciplined selection: deliberately choosing the few things that matter most and refusing the rest.

## Main Framework

Explore, Eliminate, Execute.

- Explore: create space to discern the vital few from the trivial many.
- Eliminate: cut out nonessential commitments, tasks, and options.
- Execute: remove obstacles and make progress on the essential easier.

## Key Operating Principle

### Trade-offs are unavoidable

### Paradox of Success

Success can create more options, requests, and distractions. This can diffuse effort and undermine the clarity that created the success in the first place.

### Law of the Vital Few

A small number of efforts produce most meaningful results. The practical point is to search for the few actions or commitments that matter disproportionately.

### Slowest Hiker

The slowest hiker, or Herbie, represents the constraint holding back the whole system. Under Execute, the Essentialist identifies the bottleneck and removes it instead of pushing harder everywhere.

## Application

- Create space to think, explore, and discern.
- Use extreme criteria to identify the vital few.
- Eliminate nonessentials, including existing commitments.
- Say no clearly and deliberately.
- Define an essential intent that gives direction over a longer horizon.
- Build buffers for unexpected events.
- Create routines and constraints so execution becomes easier.

## Practical Checklist

- What are my current nonessential commitments?
- Where am I saying yes by default?
- What would I uncommit from if I accepted the trade-off clearly?
- What is the essential intent for the next five years?
- What buffer do I need for the work that matters most?
