## Bank

### Survivorship Bias — *The Art of Thinking Clearly* (Dobelli)

You judge your odds by the winners you can see, but the losers who took the same path are invisible — so the sample is rigged toward success before you even start estimating.

*My example:* my high school's "best and brightest" were expected to end up famous and rich, but most of the class just didn't, and I never heard about them because no one talks about the ones who didn't make it.

*Counter:* it's only bias if I never checked the graveyard — if I actually compared, and "best and brightest" still wins, that's not bias, that's data.

### Memorization ≠ Understanding — from IELTS essay practice, not a book

A person can fully grasp *why* something is true while still failing to reproduce it on demand — retrieval and comprehension are separate skills, and testing only the first punishes people who genuinely have the second.

*My example:* a chemistry student understands why a reaction occurs but freezes trying to recall the exact formula under exam pressure — real understanding, unrecognized because the test only checked recall.

*Counter:* recall isn't nothing — in fields where the exact fact matters immediately (a nurse's drug dosage, a pilot's checklist), understanding the principle without producing the number fast enough is a real failure, not just a flaw in the test.
