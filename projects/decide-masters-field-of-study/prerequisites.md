# Prerequisites for the Two Fields

Project: [[projects/decide-masters-field-of-study/decide-masters-field-of-study|Decide Master's Field of Study]]

Step 4, researched 2026-10-06. The transcript half is still open.

**The two fields are not equally reachable from software.**

## Chip Design (MSEE / VLSI) — bridge required

A CS or software bachelor's counts as a non-EE background. Programs admit such applicants but attach makeup coursework: NJIT lists 18 credits of bridge (circuits and systems II, signals and systems, random signals, electromagnetic fields and waves, electronics II), and Columbia runs a standing EE MS Bridge Program built for exactly this case.

Assumed before any of that: mathematics through multivariable calculus, and calculus-based physics with lab.

So: reachable, but add a bridge year and its cost to every chip design program.

## Robotics — directly reachable

CS is listed as an accepted feeder background, not a deficiency. What programs expect:

- Linear algebra, multivariable calculus, ordinary differential equations, probability and statistics.
- Programming in C++ or Python — already there.
- Controls is taught in the curriculum; prior background is a bonus, not an admission bar.

## What This Does to the Decision

The two costs run opposite to the two benefits. Chip design buys the clean break from software but charges a bridge year. Robotics admits you as you are but delivers a degree that is substantially code — less of the break the step 1 sentence says the degree must buy.

## Still Open

Against the undergrad transcript: multivariable calculus, calculus-based physics with lab, linear algebra, ODEs, probability and statistics. Each missing one is a bridge course before applying.
