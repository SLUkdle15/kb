# Article Rules

Check a sentence against these two before moving to the next one. Both are repeat categories in [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/error-log|the error log]], distilled from the 08-31 self-score.

## A Singular Countable Noun Cannot Stand Alone

If the noun is singular and countable, it needs `a`, `an`, `the`, or a possessive/demonstrative in front of it — no exceptions.

- ❌ "a household performed average of 50 hours" → ✅ "a household performed **an** average of 50 hours"
- ❌ "I have chance to learn before" → ✅ "I have **a** chance to learn before"
- ❌ "Second option is to ban heavy duty transportations" → ✅ "**A** second option is to ban heavy-duty transportation"

Scan habit: after writing a sentence, check every singular noun for an article or possessive right before it.

## Superlatives Always Take *the*

*The most, the least, the highest, the fastest* — a superlative names the one extreme out of three or more, so it is always definite.

- ❌ "42%, highest among three" → ✅ "42%, **the** highest of the three"

Compare the three comparison forms, since Task 1 uses all of them: *high* (positive, no comparison) → *higher than* (comparative, 2 things, no article) → ***the* highest of/among** (superlative, 3+ things, always *the*).
