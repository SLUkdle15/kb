### The Pass

- **Cue card coverage.** All four Part 2 slots are present — where (Ta Chi Nhu, north of Hanoi), who (a colleague), what (the climb, the porters, the sunset, the hot pot dinner), why memorable (finishing the hike, the shared silence at sunset). Structurally complete despite the delivery being rough.
- **Fluency.** Filler density is very high — "uh"/"um" land every few words, with frequent false starts and self-correction ("we start on, uh, s- uh, Friday night", "finally take charge of the--", "their experie- their experience"). Against this, the candidate is clearly willing to speak at length and never gives up on an answer or shortens it. That combination — sustained length, but constant repair — sits at Band 5.5 rather than 5 or 6. Note: "mark where the silences fell" from the checklist needs actual audio; a transcript alone can't place pauses.
- **Lexical Resource.** Some good items land — *heritage*, *porters*, *precious time* — but common words repeat without paraphrase (*enjoy*, *memorable*, *trip*), and there are noticeable collocation errors: *"a healing up times"* for something like "some time to recharge", *"it was very achieve"* (wrong word class — needed a noun/adjective like "rewarding"), *"finally take charge of the trip"* for "finally set off". Band 5: limited but task-adequate range, with collocation slips that cost the listener some effort.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy.** Two errors match categories already in [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/error-log|Error Log]] from the Writing baselines: subject-verb agreement (*"it give me"*) and missing plural -s (*"one of the highest mountain"*). A third pattern is new: past tense drops out mid-narrative — *"we start... end up... return to Hanoi"*, *"we... have a plan... finally saving time"* — at least four slips in one short stretch, once the story moves past the opening "I went hiking" clause. Complex-clause attempts also come out garbled (*"I actually finished, uh, which is I made it to the top"*). Band 5: basic sentences under control, subordination attempted but unreliable, frequent errors.

### What to Fix First

1. **Do an actual audio pass for Pronunciation**, when there's time — the 5 currently on the books is an assumption, not a listen.
2. **Subject-verb agreement and plural -s**, the same two categories the Writing baselines already flagged — now confirmed as a speaking issue too, not just a writing one.
3. **Hold past tense through a full narrative.** New pattern: once the story gets past "I went hiking," tense reverts to present for several clauses in a row. Different from the Task 1 baseline's "present tense for historical chart data" — this is narrative past tense dropped mid-story, not a report-tense confusion.
4. **Cut filler density.** Some hesitation is normal in spontaneous speech, but this transcript's rate is high enough to be its own Fluency cost, independent of the self-corrections.
5. **Record and score a Part 3 answer** before the diagnostic verdict — this recording never reaches it, and Part 3 tests a different register (abstract discussion, not narrative) than the Part 2 story.
