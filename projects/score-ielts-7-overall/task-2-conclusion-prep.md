## Position Plus the Reasons, Nothing New

> In conclusion, [the answer], since [reason one, compressed] and [reason two, compressed].

The two ends of the range are both in the log:

**Worked (09-03, Task Response ~6.5–7):**

> *In conclusion, I agree the argument written exams are not best measure of ability since they penalize strong understanding delivered imperfectly on paper, while alternatives like presentations assess a fuller, more practically relevant set of skills.*

Position, then both reasons named in compressed form. Nothing from the prompt is re-quoted.

**Failed (09-07):**

> *In conclusion, despite both views that whether students should be taught a sense of competition or cooperation are beneficial, I prefer the second view due to its important.*

The prompt's full wording is dragged back in, which is where the garbling happens — and after all that, no reason is given at all. *Due to its important* is a placeholder standing where the argument should be.

The rule the working one follows: the conclusion carries the position and the reasons **already made**, in fewer words than they were first made in. Re-cramming the prompt is the failure mode to watch, since it has now caused the same breakage twice in one essay.

One sentence if it fits, two at most. Around 40 words at the word budget in the hub note.

## Checklist

- [ ] The conclusion names the position **and** each reason given in the bodies.
- [ ] The conclusion does not re-quote the prompt's wording.
- [ ] The conclusion introduces no idea the bodies never argued.
- [ ] The position is the same one stated in the introduction.
- [ ] *that* is present after *the argument* and *I agree* — the pattern with the most entries in [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/error-log|Error Log]].
