# Task 2 Intro and Conclusion Prep

The shape both ends of a Task 2 essay share, and what each type needs in each slot. The detail lives in two notes:

- [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/task-2-intro-prep|Task 2 Introduction Prep]] — the two openers, the one-formula diagnosis, sentence 1 and sentence 2, and the two-part question.
- [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/task-2-conclusion-prep|Task 2 Conclusion Prep]] — the worked and failed conclusions, and the rule between them.

Distilled from the four Task 2 reps in [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/practice-essays|Practice Essays]] and the 09-07 self-score; the band-level reasoning behind it is levers 1, 2, and 4 in [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/writing-6-5-levers|Writing 6.5 Levers]].

## The Shape

Two sentences in, one or two out, for every one of the five types. What changes between types is not the shape — it is what counts as an answer.

**Intro, sentence 1 — paraphrase the prompt.** The situation, in different words. No opinion, no reason, no new information. Where the prompt raises two sides, both go here, each as a claim somebody holds.

**Intro, sentence 2 — answer the question actually asked, with the content named.** Not *I will discuss two advantages*; the two advantages, named. This is the sentence the whole essay is then held to.

**Conclusion — the same answer, plus the reasons already given, compressed.** One sentence if it fits, two at most. Nothing enters here that the bodies did not argue, and the prompt's wording does not come back.

> In conclusion, [the answer], since [reason one, compressed] and [reason two, compressed].

What "the answer" means per type:

| Type | Sentence 1 carries | Sentence 2 carries | Conclusion restates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opinion (agree/disagree) | the claim being judged | your position, plus the reasons named | the position and its reasons |
| Discuss both views | both views, each as a claim | your position, with the side named | the position, with at most one clause granting the other side |
| Problem and solution | the situation | the problems named, and the solutions that answer them | the problems' core and the recommended solution |
| Advantages and disadvantages | the change or situation | the advantage and the disadvantage, named | both, plus a verdict **only** if the prompt asked whether one outweighs the other |
| Two-part question | the situation | a direct answer to each of the two parts | both answers |

Two things the shape cannot do, worth knowing before trusting it:

- **It cannot rescue the wrong type.** Answering one part of a two-part question caps Task Response at 5 whatever the intro looks like — lever 1 in [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/writing-6-5-levers|Writing 6.5 Levers]]. This is a container; naming the type correctly is still the first move.
- **The paraphrase has to be real.** Copied prompt wording is discounted from the word count, which matters at 217 and 241 words. Whether a memorised *frame* is also discounted is an open question — see below.

**Word budget at this shape.** Intro about 45 words, two bodies about 85 each, conclusion about 40 — roughly 255, which clears the 250 minimum with nothing to spare. Both recent essays came in under. The intro and conclusion are the two places where words go to announcements instead of arguments, which is where the missing 35 words have been going.

## Open Question — How Far the Frame Rule Goes

[[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/materials|Materials]], under What to Ignore, rules out "full essay templates with fixed opening phrases," on the grounds that examiners discount memorised language so a templated introduction contributes nothing and eats words.

These notes assume that rule targets fixed *phrasing* rather than a reusable decision about what content goes in which slot. That reading is not what Materials actually says, and it was not the rule's author who made the distinction. Resolve it in one direction or the other — tighten Materials to say fixed phrases specifically, or drop the frame claim from the intro note — because it decides how much of the intro note to trust.
