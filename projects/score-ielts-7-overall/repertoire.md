# Repertoire

**Not an idea bank.** The old version stored pre-written arguments per topic and the 09-07 rep showed why that fails: the one prompt on an unstocked topic dropped Lexical Resource back to the baseline rate, because there is no way to stock every topic the exam might hand over. This note stores the two things that do transfer — a way to *generate* content on any topic, and a handful of concrete examples too specific to invent under the clock.

Its counterpart is [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/error-log|Error Log]]: that note is what to stop doing, this one is how to find something to say.

## How to Use It

1. **Name the family** the prompt belongs to.
2. **Pick one or two lenses** — not all of them. Two lenses is a two-body essay.
3. **Ask the matching question out loud** and answer it in plain English, badly.
4. **Then** convert the plain answer to academic phrasing. Ideas first, language second; doing both at once is what produces *due to its important*.

The lenses are for finding an idea, not for wording it. Nothing here is a phrase to reuse.

## The Lens Card

### Education

All four Task 2 reps so far have been education or exams — see [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/practice-essays|Practice Essays]] — so this is the family to be fastest on.

**Lenses:** what it's meant to measure vs. what it measures, exams vs. life after school, who decides (school / parent / student), access and cost

**Questions:**

- What is this actually meant to produce or measure, and does it?
- Does this prepare someone for the exam, or for what comes after it?
- Who's making the decision here — the school, the parent, or the student?
- Who is priced or filtered out of this, and what do they get instead?

### Individual and Society

*Celebrities, privacy, lifestyle, personal choices.*

**Lenses:** consent/choice, boundaries, power/harm, fairness/comparison

**Questions:**

- Did this person choose the situation they're in, or was it forced on them?
- Where's the line between their public role and their private life?
- Who actually gets hurt here, and how badly?
- Would we treat an ordinary person the same way in this situation?

### Environment

**Lenses:** economic cost vs. environmental cost, individual vs. government responsibility, short-term vs. long-term, feasibility

**Questions:**

- What does fixing this actually cost, and who pays?
- Is this something one person can meaningfully affect, or does it need policy?
- What happens if we do nothing for 20 more years?
- Is the ideal solution actually realistic to implement?

### Technology

**Lenses:** convenience vs. dependency, job displacement, human connection, access/inequality

**Questions:**

- What does this make easier — and what skill or habit does that convenience quietly replace?
- What jobs does this threaten, and what jobs does it create?
- Does this bring people together or isolate them?
- Who can't afford or access this, and what happens to them?

### Government and Society

*Crime, law, public funding.*

**Lenses:** individual rights vs. collective good, prevention vs. punishment, cost/resources, effectiveness

**Questions:**

- Does this protect the group at the cost of individual freedom, or the reverse?
- Does this stop the problem before it happens, or only punish it afterwards?
- Is this actually affordable at scale?
- Does the evidence show it works, or does it just sound good?

### Work

**Lenses:** wellbeing vs. productivity, skill relevance, career progression, work-life balance

**Questions:**

- Does this help output, or help the person doing the work — or both?
- Will this skill still matter in 10 years?
- Does this help someone grow into a better role, or just complete a task?
- Does this eat into someone's personal life?

### Universal Fallback

For any prompt where no family above fits.

- Who benefits, and who is harmed?
- Short-term effect vs. long-term effect?
- What does the real world already do about this — name a country, system, or policy.

## Anchor Examples

**Not yet built.** Five or six real, specific things, reusable across several families. This is the scarce half of the work: arguments improvise fine under time, the concrete example does not come when the clock is running, and band 7 asks for it. An example from your own life doubles as Speaking Part 2 material.

What counts as an anchor:

- **Real and specific.** A named place, policy, system, or something that actually happened to you. Not *studies show* and not an invented person.
- **One sentence of detail** you can produce cold — the detail is what makes it evidence rather than an assertion.
- **Reusable across families.** An anchor that only fits one prompt is not worth the storage. A congestion charge serves Environment, Government, and Work.
- **Cap at six.** A bank too big to re-read before a session never gets used. Replace the weakest rather than adding a seventh.
