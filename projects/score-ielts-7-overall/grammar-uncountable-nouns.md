# Uncountable Nouns

Never take `-s`. The other half of the plural problem: [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/error-log|the error log]] shows plural failing in both directions, and this list is the *wrongly added* direction. The missing-`-s`-on-countables direction stays in the log as corrected sentences.

Check a noun against this list before pluralising it. These are the ones that have come up or are likely to, given the essay topics.

- data
- transportation
- equipment
- furniture
- luggage
- traffic
- information
- advice
- news
- homework
- research
- evidence
- progress
- accommodation

*transportations* has already appeared in a real essay, *a data* appeared on 2026-08-29, and *traffic* recurs across the congestion topics — those three are the live risks. *Data* matters most: it is the opening noun of almost every Task 1.

**They take a singular verb.** *The equipment is expensive*, not *are*. *Traffic has worsened*, not *have*. *This research shows*, not *show*. This matters twice over: subject-verb agreement is already the longest run of entries in [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/error-log|the error log]], so a wrongly pluralised uncountable usually drags a wrong verb along with it and costs two errors in one sentence.

To count them, count the container, not the noun: *a piece of equipment*, *two items of luggage*, *a great deal of progress* — and the verb then follows the container: *two items of luggage were lost*.
