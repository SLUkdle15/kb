# Writing 6.5 Levers

Writing is scored on four criteria, weighted equally: Task Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1.

An essay sitting at 5.5 is almost never failing on all four. Find which one is dragging, because averaging four criteria means one 5 costs the same as one 8 gains.

## What Separates 6 from 7

Paraphrased from the public band descriptors, which are worth reading in full once:

- **Task Response.** Band 6 addresses all parts but the position becomes unclear or repetitive and some main ideas are underdeveloped. Band 7 holds a clear position *throughout* and extends and supports each main idea.
- **Coherence and Cohesion.** Band 6 is coherent overall but cohesion is mechanical or faulty between sentences, and paragraphing is not always logical. Band 7 gives every paragraph one clear central topic and uses linking without over- or under-using it.
- **Lexical Resource.** Band 6 has enough vocabulary but errors in word choice and formation. Band 7 has enough range for flexibility and precision, with *some* less common items used with awareness of collocation.
- **Grammatical Range and Accuracy.** Band 6 mixes simple and complex forms and makes errors that rarely impede. Band 7 produces **frequent error-free sentences** and good control of punctuation.

That last line is the most useful sentence in the whole descriptor set. Band 7 grammar is not about complexity, it is about frequent error-free sentences. Which means the fastest route up is writing sentences that are certainly correct, not sentences that are ambitiously complex. A short correct sentence outscores a long broken one, every time.

## The Five Things Worth Fixing First

1. **Answer the actual question type.** Five types recur: opinion (agree/disagree), discuss both views, problem and solution, advantages and disadvantages, and the two-part question. Answering a two-part question while only addressing one part caps Task Response at 5, and no amount of good English rescues it. Before writing, name the type out loud and name what a complete answer must contain.

2. **State the position in the introduction and never drift from it.** "Presents a clear position throughout" is the band 7 phrase. A body paragraph that argues the other side without framing it as a concession reads as an unclear position. Pick a side in the introduction, keep it in every paragraph, restate it in the conclusion.

3. **One idea per paragraph, developed.** The band 6 trap is three shallow reasons; band 7 wants fewer ideas taken further. The reliable shape per body paragraph: topic sentence stating the idea, one sentence explaining *why* it is true, one concrete example, one sentence on why it matters for the position. Two body paragraphs done that way beat four done thinly.

4. **Kill the mechanical linkers.** "Firstly, Secondly, Moreover, In conclusion" as a skeleton is exactly the mechanical cohesion band 6 describes. Replace most of them with referencing — *this shift*, *such policies*, *the same pressure* — which is what band 7 means by using cohesive devices appropriately.

5. **Never sacrifice accuracy for ambition.** With grammar at band 5, adding relative clauses and inversions adds errors faster than range. Master four structures cold and use them everywhere: a defining relative clause, a conditional, a passive for processes, and a contrast clause with *while* or *whereas*. That is enough complex range for band 7 if the sentences are clean.

## Task 1 Specifically

- **The overview sentence is mandatory.** No overview means Task Achievement cannot pass 5. Write it second, right after the paraphrased introduction, and start it with a plain "Overall,".
- **Select, do not list.** Band 6 describes every number; band 7 selects the key features and compares them. Two or three body sentences with the biggest contrast beats an exhaustive tour of the data.
- **Twenty minutes, hard stop.** Task 1 is worth a third of the Writing score. Running to 30 minutes trades a small Task 1 gain for a large Task 2 loss.

## Timing on the Day

Do Task 2 first, then Task 1. Task 2 is worth double, and an unfinished conclusion damages Task Response directly, whereas a slightly thin Task 1 costs less. Cap Task 2 at 40 minutes even mid-sentence, then move.

Minimums: 250 words for Task 2, 150 for Task 1. Under-length is penalised outright. Learn what 250 words looks like in the computer-delivered word counter so it never needs counting.

## The Self-Scoring Pass

Run this on every essay, straight after writing it and before any rewrite. Twenty minutes.

- [ ] Name the question type. Did the essay answer that type, and every part of it?
- [ ] Underline the position in the introduction. Is it still recognisably the same position in each body paragraph and the conclusion?
- [ ] Mark each body paragraph's topic sentence. Any paragraph without one, or with two ideas in it, is a Coherence problem.
- [ ] For each main idea: is there a *why* and a concrete example? Unsupported ideas cap Task Response at 6.
- [ ] Count the linkers. More than one per paragraph opening is usually over-use.
- [ ] Count sentences, then count error-free sentences. The ratio is the Grammatical Accuracy score in disguise. Under half means grammar is the binding constraint and the answer is simpler sentences, not more.
- [ ] Circle every word chosen for effect rather than meaning. Check each against a collocation dictionary — a wrong collocation costs more in Lexical Resource than a plain word would have.
- [ ] Check the word count.
- [ ] Add every repeated error to [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/error-log|Error Log]]. Repeated, not one-off.
- [ ] Give a band per criterion, then rewrite the essay targeting only the lowest one.

An AI grader is useful for the mechanical half of this — spotting grammar errors, missing parts of the question, unsupported claims. Treat its band numbers as noise; they are generous and inconsistent. Trust the criterion-level feedback, not the score.
