# Six-Week Plan

Built for 5–7 hours a week — about an hour on weekdays and a longer block on Saturday. Test date 2026-10-03.

The shape of it: two weeks fixing mechanics, two weeks of volume with rewrites, one week under exam conditions, one week tapering. Writing and Speaking alternate on weekdays so neither gets a week off. Reading and Listening get one Saturday section, purely to hold the band.

## The Weekly Template

| Day | Block | Work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | 60 min | Task 2: 5 min plan, 35 min write to time, 20 min self-score against the descriptors |
| Tue | 60 min | Speaking Part 2: three cue cards, each recorded to a full two minutes, then listen back to one |
| Wed | 60 min | Rewrite Monday's essay from the self-score. Update [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/error-log|Error Log]] |
| Thu | 60 min | Speaking Part 3 drills, then 10 min pronunciation (final consonants, word stress) |
| Fri | 60 min | Task 1 to time (20 min), then read two model answers closely and steal the sentence patterns |
| Sat | 90–120 min | Alternating: full Writing paper under 60 min, or a timed Reading or Listening section |
| Sun | rest | Optional 20 min re-reading the error log |

Wednesday is the highest-value hour in the week. Writing new essays raises volume; rewriting the same essay after seeing what was wrong with it is what actually moves the band. Never skip Wednesday to write a fresh essay instead.

## Week 0 — 19 to 23 August: Baseline

Nothing improves until the diagnosis is real.

- Book the test. Slots and centre choice both matter — see [[next/next-actions/2026-08-19 - Book the IELTS Test for Early October]].
- Read the official public band descriptors for Writing Task 2 and for Speaking, and read the 6 and 7 rows side by side. Twenty minutes, and it reframes everything after it.
- Sit a baseline: one Task 2 in 40 minutes, one Task 1 in 20, one recorded full Speaking test. Self-score all of it. See [[next/next-actions/2026-08-19 - Run the IELTS Baseline and Self-Score It]].
- One timed Reading section to confirm it is still at 7.5 or above. If it is not, the plan changes and Reading rejoins the weekday rotation.
- Start the error log with whatever the baseline exposes.

Read the baseline honestly. Structural failures — no clear position, no Task 1 overview, Part 2 answers running dry at 50 seconds, under 250 words — are six-week problems. If instead the writing addresses the question fully and the speaking runs the full two minutes and it still scores 5.5 on grammar and vocabulary alone, that is a slower problem, and the right move is a November or December test date rather than a heroic October.

## Weeks 1–2 — 24 August to 6 September: Mechanics

Fix form before adding volume. Writing badly in bulk just automates the mistakes.

- Drill the five Task 2 question types until reading the prompt reliably produces the right essay shape: opinion, discuss-both-views, problem and solution, advantages and disadvantages, two-part question. Answering an opinion question with a discussion essay caps Task Response at 5 no matter how good the English is.
- Lock one four-paragraph Task 2 structure and stop redesigning it.
- Task 1: the overview sentence becomes non-negotiable. No overview caps Task Achievement at 5.
- Speaking Part 2: a full two minutes, every single time, using the four-slot frame in [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/speaking-6-5-levers|Speaking 6.5 Levers]].
- Saturday of week 1: full Writing paper. Saturday of week 2: timed Listening section.
- Target by 6 September: four Task 2 essays written and rewritten, two Task 1s, roughly twenty recorded Part 2 turns, and an error log with real repeat offenders in it.

## Weeks 3–4 — 7 to 20 September: Volume and Rewrite

Form is set; now build reps and hunt accuracy.

- Two Task 2 plus one Task 1 per week, each rewritten once. Rotate topics across education, technology, environment, health, work, crime and law, government spending, and family — the recurring themes.
- Speaking moves to Part 3, which is where 6.5 is won or lost. Two-sentence answers read as band 5 regardless of accent or vocabulary.
- Build the topic vocabulary list: forty to sixty items with collocations, not a thousand flashcards. Band 7 asks for *some* less common vocabulary used precisely, not exotic vocabulary used wrongly.
- Saturday 12 September: **first full mock** — all four sections, correct order, no pauses. This is the real checkpoint. If Writing and Speaking self-score at 6.0 here, week 5 goes entirely to whichever criterion is dragging.
- Saturday 19 September: full Writing paper plus a Reading section.

## Week 5 — 21 to 27 September: Exam Conditions

- Saturday 26 September: **second full mock**, morning, same time of day as the real test.
- Everything else this week runs to the clock with no restarts. Timing failures, not language failures, are what turn a 6.5 essay into a 5.5 one — an unfinished conclusion damages Task Response directly.
- Attack the weakest single criterion from the mock. One criterion, not four.
- Re-read every essay written so far in one sitting. The repeated errors become obvious in bulk in a way they never are one essay at a time.

## Week 6 — 28 September to 2 October: Taper

No new material. No new structures. No new vocabulary lists.

- Two short Speaking sessions to stay warm, twenty minutes each.
- One Task 1 and one Task 2 plan-only session — outline and topic sentences, no full essay.
- Read the error log daily. It is short by now and it is the highest-yield thing left.
- Confirm the test logistics: centre address, ID document, report time.
- Sleep properly the two nights before. Fluency and Coherence is the band most visibly wrecked by fatigue.

## After the Test

- Results in 1–5 days for computer-delivered.
- All four bands at target: this project is done, and step 5 of [[projects/pursue-a-masters-degree/pursue-a-masters-degree|Pursue a Master's Degree]] clears.
- Exactly one of Writing or Speaking below 6.5: book the One Skill Retake inside the 60-day window.
- Both below: book a full retake and rebuild the plan from the new score report, which will be far more specific than any self-scoring done here.
