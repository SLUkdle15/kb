# Vocabulary

Topic collocations captured while writing, not a "band 9 vocabulary" list. [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/materials|Materials]] rules those out — forty to sixty collocations actually used in an essay beat a thousand reviewed flashcards.

Each entry: the collocation, the correction if one was needed, and one example sentence.

## General / Any Topic

- **see little value in** (+ gerund/noun), not *value of*. *Many people see little value in studying the past.*
- **provide limited practical guidance for understanding [X]** — downplays how useful one thing is for grasping another. *Looking backwards provides limited practical guidance for understanding what is happening now.*
- **fall behind** (+ peers, unmodified, or *schedule*) — the standard collocation for losing ground relative to others; no preposition needed before the group. *These students fall behind not through any lack of effort, but because a laptop has quietly become the price of admission to their own education.*
- **[X] that relies on [Y] has no control when [Y] is cut off** — a dependency argument that works for any topic about relying on an outside source (food, energy, foreign workers, a single supplier). It needs the passive *is cut off*; *when Y cut off* is missing its verb. *A country that relies on foreign farms has no control when supply is cut off.*

## History / Society

- **predict future outcomes** — plural unless naming one specific result; *predict future outcome* (singular) reads as a slip, not a choice.

## Education / Child Development

- **settle into [X] with far less disruption** — *settle into*, not "settle in education." *Children who start early tend to settle into formal education with far less disruption than those who begin later.*
- **become familiar with** vs. **accustomed to** — near-synonyms, not interchangeable partners. *Familiar with* pairs naturally with routines, subjects, material; *accustomed to* pairs with conditions, settings, environments. *Children who are already accustomed to a structured setting become familiar with academic routines faster.*

## Education / Technology

- **shut out of [X]** — names exclusion as an effect on a person, not just an absence of access. *Students without access to a laptop are shut out of online courses altogether.*
- **classroom interaction** — attributive noun, no possessive; not *classroom's interaction*. *Students who value classroom interaction often lose interest once classes move online.*

Reviewed alongside [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/task-2-writing-prep|Task 2 Writing Prep]] before writing.

## Food / Trade

- **a growing share of what [nations] eat** — a noun-phrase paraphrase of *a large amount of food*. It reworks the sentence structure rather than swapping in a synonym for *food*, so the topic noun stays available for the bodies. *A growing share of what many nations eat is now grown on other continents.*
- **other continents** — paraphrase of *other parts of the world*.
- **shipped in / shipped across borders** — paraphrase of *imported*. Passive, so the food is the subject. *Food is increasingly shipped across borders rather than grown at home.*
