# Materials

Short on purpose. The failure mode in IELTS self-study is collecting resources instead of writing essays, and every hour spent choosing a course is an hour not spent on the [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/six-week-plan|weekly template]]. Six and a half weeks does not have room for a second opinion on materials.

## Non-Negotiable

- **The official public band descriptors** for Writing Task 1, Writing Task 2, and Speaking. Free PDFs from ielts.org. Read the band 6 and band 7 rows side by side once, then keep them open during every self-scoring pass. Everything in this project is downstream of these.
- **Cambridge IELTS past paper books, 16 to 20.** Real retired tests. These are the only source of tasks worth timing yourself against; invented practice questions are subtly off in ways that matter. Use them for Reading and Listening sections too.
- **A phone voice recorder.** The entire Speaking plan runs on it.

## Writing

- **ielts-simon.com** — the free archive of model Task 2 answers, written by a former examiner. Read them for sentence patterns to steal, not for content to memorise.
- **Pauline Cullen, *The Key to IELTS Writing Task 2*** — the one book worth buying if any is. Cullen writes about the descriptors from the inside.
- **Cambridge *Grammar for IELTS* (Diana Hopkins)** — only for the specific structures the error log keeps flagging. Do not work through it front to back; there is no time and no need.
- **ozdic.com or the Oxford Learner's Dictionary** — collocation checking during the self-scoring pass. A wrong collocation costs more in Lexical Resource than a plain word would have.

## Speaking

- **IELTS Liz** — clean explanations of each part's format and a large topic bank. Best used for cue cards.
- **Keith Speaking Academy (YouTube)** — the strongest free material on Part 3 depth, which is where 6.5 is won.
- **YouGlish** — hear any word pronounced in real speech, in context. Better than a dictionary clip for word stress.
- **Minimal-pair drills for final consonants** — any source will do. The drill matters more than the source. See [[projects/score-ielts-7-overall/speaking-6-5-levers|Speaking 6.5 Levers]].

## AI as a Grader

Useful for the mechanical half of the self-scoring pass: spotting grammar errors, catching an unanswered part of the question, flagging unsupported claims. Paste the public descriptors in and ask it to mark strictly against them, criterion by criterion.

Treat the band number it gives as noise. AI graders run generous and inconsistent, and a fake 7.0 in week two is worse than no feedback at all. Trust the criterion-level comments; ignore the score.

## What to Ignore

- "Band 9 vocabulary" lists and idiom collections. Rare words used imprecisely score *below* plain words used correctly.
- Full essay templates with fixed opening phrases. Examiners discount memorised language, so a templated introduction contributes nothing and eats words.
- Anything promising 8.0 in two weeks, and any paid "predicted questions" pack.
- Vocabulary apps with streaks and points. Forty to sixty topic collocations, written by hand and actually used in an essay, beat a thousand reviewed flashcards.
- A second course, a second book, or a second method, once week one has started. Switching plans mid-project is the most expensive move available.
