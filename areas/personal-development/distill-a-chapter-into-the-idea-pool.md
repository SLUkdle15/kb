# Distill a Chapter into the Idea Pool

Source: [[areas/personal-development/2026-08-21 - Build an Idea Pool for Conversation and Debate|Build an Idea Pool for Conversation and Debate]]

## Checklist

- [ ] Read one self-contained chapter (e.g. one of the 99 in *The Art of Thinking Clearly*).
- [ ] Ask: is this a reusable stock idea that applies across multiple topics, not just one?
- [ ] Ask: what concrete example or story of my own could carry it?
- [ ] Ask: does it give me a counterargument, so an opinion sounds balanced rather than one-sided?
- [ ] Ask: can I say it in one plain sentence, fast, without hedging or hunting for vocabulary?
- [ ] Ask: does it explain a cause or a consequence I could use to build an argument?
- [ ] Write one line: the concept plus my own example, not the book's example.
- [ ] Log that line in the idea pool bank, not just the reading list.

## Notes

- Output beats reading. The written line is the deliverable; chapters read with no line produce trivia, not fluency.
- Stop once the line is written. Depth stays in the book — do not re-summarize the chapter.
- For *The Art of Thinking Clearly* specifically: chapters are independent, so a few a night costs nothing and stopping mid-book loses nothing.
