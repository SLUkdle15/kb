# Start a New Habit

Source: [[resources/behavior-change/2026-07-29 - Four Laws of Behavior Change|Four Laws of Behavior Change]]

## Checklist

- [ ] Name the identity this habit proves: "I am someone who ..." — work backward from the goal.
- [ ] Write the cue: "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]", or stack it: "After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."
- [ ] Make it attractive: bundle it ("After [HABIT I NEED], I will [HABIT I WANT]") and/or join a group where the behavior is normal.
- [ ] Cut it down to a version that takes two minutes or less, below the point where it feels like work.
- [ ] Prime the environment beforehand to remove friction, and automate or lock in what a one-time decision can cover.
- [ ] Attach an immediate reward that fires right after the habit (e.g. a savings transfer for skipped spending).
- [ ] If this is a top-priority habit, track it — log each rep immediately after it happens.
- [ ] When it plateaus, recalibrate to just-manageable difficulty instead of waiting for motivation.

## Notes

- Reps encode the habit; planning does not. Don't stall searching for the optimal plan.
- If the habit keeps dying, check for an identity conflict first ([[resources/behavior-change/2026-07-29 - Identity Change Drives Lasting Habits|Identity Change Drives Lasting Habits]]).
