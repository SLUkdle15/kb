# Oil an Electric Fan

Source: [[archives/next-actions/2026-10-03 - Clean the Electric Fan|Clean the Electric Fan]]

## Checklist

- [ ] Unplug the fan.
- [ ] Remove the front guard: unclip the guard ring, or undo its screw.
- [ ] Remove the blade: unscrew the spinner cap at the center (often reverse-threaded, so turn it clockwise to loosen), then pull the blade off.
- [ ] Remove the rear guard: unscrew the plastic nut that holds it to the motor.
- [ ] Oil the front bearing: 2–3 drops on the shaft where it enters the motor housing, then spin the shaft by hand.
- [ ] Oil the rear bearing: remove the back motor cover screws to expose the rear of the shaft, or use the small oil hole if the model has one, and add 2–3 drops.
- [ ] Spin the shaft by hand for a minute, then stand the fan motor-down for a few minutes so the oil soaks into the bushings.
- [ ] Wipe off excess oil so it doesn't collect dust.
- [ ] Reassemble, and tighten the spinner cap securely (counter-clockwise if reverse-threaded).
- [ ] Test it: no rattle or cracking sound.

## Notes

- A loose spinner cap makes a cracking sound when the fan runs. If you hear it, tighten the cap first.
- Stiff shaft: oil it, wait 10–15 minutes, and repeat.
- Still won't spin freely, or hums but won't turn: the start capacitor may be faulty. It's a cheap part to replace.
